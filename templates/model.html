Our Machine Learning Model

A comparison of machine-learning approaches for classifying astronomical observations from NASA’s Kepler mission.

THE SELECTED APPROACH

Many trees.
A clearer signal.

Logistic Regression, Random Forest, and an Artificial Neural Network were trained and compared. Random Forest was selected based on its validation performance.

The saved pipeline combines median imputation with an ensemble of decision trees. It interprets ten astronomical measurements as a binary classification.

05 / SCIENTIFIC RESPONSIBILITY

What this model can — and cannot — tell us

  • Training data describes historical Kepler observations; performance on new observations may differ.
  • The output is a binary model classification. Candidate and Confirmed observations were grouped into the Planet-like training class.
  • Class-1 model scores are not automatically calibrated probabilities, and are not scientifically verified probabilities that planets exist.
  • The model cannot independently discover or confirm a new exoplanet. NASA catalog dispositions remain separate.
  • The final test set was inspected during development. The reported results should not be presented as an untouched independent benchmark.
